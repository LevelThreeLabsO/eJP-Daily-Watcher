System design v2 · eJP Daily Phil watchers

Bank the future instead of hunting for it every morning

The current build tries to discover today's news today. That works for gifts and fails for events, because an event is announced weeks before it happens. One change fixes it: keep a ledger.

The problem in one sentence

A conference is news twice

Once when it's announced, and again on the day it opens. The v1 system only ever looks at today's news, so it sees the announcement three weeks early — when it's useless — and then sees nothing on the morning it actually matters.

That's why What We're Watching came back with two items instead of three, and why the two it found were things that happened yesterday rather than things starting today. Major Gifts works fine on the same plumbing because a gift is only news once.

Three components

What actually gets built

1 — the ledger

A dated store, not a daily scrape

Every item that passes through intake gets read once for dated facts: what is happening, when, and to whom. Anything with a future date is banked. Each morning the digest is a query — "what's on for today, and what ends today" — not a hunt.

This is also what makes the span framing possible. A retreat with a start and end date can be described as kicked off last night and runs through Sunday, which is 21% of your real items and something a same-day scrape structurally cannot say.

2 — the entity memory

The watchlist writes itself

87% of the organizations you name appear exactly once, so no hand-built list can work. But every morning your own newsletter names three to five more. The system reads eJP's published output, extracts the organizations, and adds them to what it watches.

Start at zero and after a month it knows several hundred orgs that matter to you, including the ones I'd never have thought to list — Rohr JLI, Aid L'Shalom, 3GNY.

3 — the scorecard

It grades itself every morning

Once your edition publishes, the system compares its proposals against what you actually ran. Hits, misses, and things it suggested that you ignored. That number goes in the next day's Slack message.

A miss isn't just logged — the source that carried it gets added to intake. This is the thing whose absence caused every problem so far: I had no way to know I was wrong until you told me.

Fit

Cost and cadence

The ledger design uses fewer model calls than the current one, because each item is read once ever rather than re-evaluated daily.

Extract dated facts from new itemsevery run2–3 ~300 headlines, batched ~100 per call; only unseen items

Daily total 4–5 against a free-tier cap of 20

Run it twice a day rather than once: an early-morning pass that produces the digest before the newsletter is assembled, and an evening pass that ingests the day's news and scores. Storage is a JSON file in the repo, committed by the workflow, same as the existing cache.

Order of work

What I'd build, in what order

  1. The ledger and the scorecard together. Even with the current intake, being able to say "we would have caught 6 of your 9 items yesterday" changes every conversation after it. About a day.
  2. Backfill the ledger from the archive. Your 1,216 past editions mention hundreds of future-dated events and thousands of organizations. Seeding from that means the system starts knowing your world rather than learning it from scratch.
  3. Add the scheduled-source layer — conference sites and court dockets — but only for orgs the entity memory has already learned are yours, not a list I invent.
  4. Tune against the scorecard until the number stops moving.

Everything stays on the free Gemini tier, in the existing repo, posting to the two channels you already made. Nothing posts until the scorecard says it's worth reading.
