I built the wrong thing twice, and the evidence to know better was already on my desk

The editor's feedback on this morning's two digests was specific enough to diagnose properly. This is what went wrong, measured against eJP's own published sections rather than against my impressions — plus the process change that would have caught it before a single line of code was written.

Of 184 real items eJP published in this section, 22% could ever have been reached by an organizational events calendar — and an organizational events calendar is the only thing I built. The design ceiling was about one item in five before a single filter ran.

The measurement I should have taken first

Their archive was a labeled test set the whole time

Every Daily Phil edition carries this section — 60 out of 60 I sampled, averaging 3.1 items each. I could have pulled hundreds of real examples on day one, asked "which of these could my pipeline reach?", and gotten a number. I never did. I built from the editor's description of his manual routine and from my own assumptions about where events live.

Error one

What We're Watching is a today brief, not a calendar

I built a rolling 30-day forward calendar. The section is a same-day news brief. Those are different products, and the difference shows in the language of every real item:

Israel's Education Ministry, Ministry of Immigration and Absorption and Nefesh B'Nefesh launched the National Educator's Aliyah Program earlier today, seeking to fast-track… as the country works to fill a shortage of 4,000 teachers.

The Jewish Federations of North America's National Young Leadership Cabinet retreat concludes today in Minneapolis.

Oral arguments begin today in a federal court in Washington in National Ben Gamla Jewish Charter School Foundation, Inc. v. Drummond…

This evening, the remains of Zionist visionary Theodor Herzl's grandparents will be reinterred in Israel's Mount Herzl National Cemetery.

Read those four and count the sources. One — the JFNA retreat — could come from an organizational events page. The other three come from an Israeli ministry announcement, a federal court docket, and Israeli press. My system could not have produced three of the four items on the day the editor complained.

Three separate mistakes compound here:

  • Wrong window. I shipped events up to 30 days out. Their reader wants to know what is happening today, including what is ending today — a concept my model never even had, because a calendar entry has a start date and I never tracked spans.
  • Wrong register. Their items are sentences with news context and a stake. Mine were rows: title, date, org. Even a correct event, formatted as a calendar row, isn't usable copy.
  • Wrong altitude. I sent HUC admissions webinars, Hadassah chapter mahjong afternoons and "Baby & Me Yoga" to a channel read by people who fund national institutions. I had already written a filter for exactly this and it wasn't strict enough, because I was filtering a source that was 90% wrong rather than choosing a better source.

Error two

Major Gifts: two misses, traced through the actual code

The editor named two stories eJP ran that I didn't surface. They failed for completely different reasons, and only one of them is fixable.

Miss 1 — Bank of Jerusalem's donation to Soroka

This one is squarely my fault. The story was in Google News, from the Jerusalem Post, inside my search window. I ran the real headline back through my own filters:

"Bank of Jerusalem to donate hundreds of thousands of shekels to Soroka"

  gift language present ...... PASS
  not business news ......... PASS
  not political ............. PASS
  dollar amount parsed ...... 0  ← "hundreds of thousands" has no numeral
  Jewish-relevance match .... NONE  ← "Jerusalem", "Soroka" absent from my vocabulary
  → guard: no amount AND no Jewish angle  DROPPED

Two independent failures in one headline. My amount parser only understands numerals, so a gift described in words scores zero. And my Jewish-relevance vocabulary listed "Israel", "Israeli" and "Hebrew" but not Jerusalem — nor any Israeli city, hospital or university. Soroka is one of the largest medical centers in Israel and my code had never heard of it.

There is a third, worse bug sitting next to it. My currency filter rejects any headline containing "shekel", on the theory that non-dollar amounts are foreign noise. For a publication where Israel appears in 84% of coverage, that filter is actively hostile. It only spared this story by accident — the headline said "shekels" plural and my pattern matched the singular.

Miss 2 — the Satmar philanthropist's $1M legal aid fund

This one I want to be straight about rather than promise away. I searched for it in Google News across four query formulations: zero results. I then pulled 250 headlines from 12 Jewish, Israeli and haredi feeds — JNS, JPost, JTA, Forward, Algemeiner, Ynet, Matzav, Yeshiva World News, Yated, Jewish Press, Collive and eJP itself. The story is in none of them.

So it most likely reached eJP the way my own audit said most of their news arrives: directly. The audit found 73% of eJP news stories quote an interview eJP conducted itself, and the editor told us plainly that organizations announcing a big gift "are gonna send it to us anyway." A real share of Major Gifts will never be publicly scrapeable, and a tool that claims otherwise is lying.

What I should do is add the haredi press properly — five of those feeds work and none were in the build — and then be honest that the inbox-sourced items are the editor's, not the machine's.

And the noise I did send

Memphis Zoo's $50M matching gift. A $5.5M gift to protect a bald eagle's habitat. Those outranked Jewish items because I sorted by dollar size and treated Jewish relevance as a label applied afterwards. It should be a gate applied first. A $200M gift with no Jewish angle is worth less to this newsletter than a $1M gift with one, and my ranking asserted the opposite.

The root cause

I did the research, then ignored it

This is the part worth dwelling on, because the filter bugs are symptoms.

Three days ago I read all 1,204 of eJP's news articles and wrote up what the archive showed. Among the findings:

Israel appears in 84% of all news stories. The most-linked domains are Jewish press — Jewish Insider, Times of Israel, JTA, Jerusalem Post, Haaretz, Forward, Ynet, JNS. Hebrew-language business press barely appears: Calcalist five stories, Globes four.
— my own audit of eJP's news archive, 3 Aug

Then I built a gift finder whose sources were generic English-language Google News searches for American universities, hospitals and museums, and an events aggregator built on US federation calendars. Not one Israeli outlet. Not one haredi outlet. No Hebrew. I had measured that this publication is overwhelmingly about Israel and then built two systems that structurally could not see Israel.

Why: I took the editor's description of his manual workflow — "I type in university million dollar donation, hospital million dollar donation" — as the specification. It isn't the specification. It is the workaround he uses because he has no better tool, and it covers the one slice of the beat that generic search happens to reach. Automating someone's workaround reproduces its blind spots at scale.

The same mistake produced the events failure. I asked "where do event listings live?" and answered "organizational calendars", which is true and nearly irrelevant. The right question was "where do the items in this section actually come from?" — answerable in one minute from their archive, and the answer is Israeli press and news, not calendars.

The fix

What changes

What We're Watching · sources

Add the 13 verified feeds — JPost (60 items/pull), JNS, JTA, Forward, Algemeiner, Ynet, plus the five haredi outlets. These carry the Israeli ministry announcements, the reinterments, the primary-day items and the Monsey benefits that calendars never will.

Add CourtListener for a tracked-case docket calendar: I confirmed it holds National Ben Gamla Jewish Charter School Foundation v. Drummond with its filing date. Only 2% of items are court-derived, so this is small and cheap, not a pillar.

Keep conference calendars, but only the national ones, and track end dates so "concludes today" becomes possible. Israeli government sites block scraping (gov.il returns 403), so ministry news arrives via the press feeds instead.

What We're Watching · shape

Window collapses from 30 days to today, plus what ends today and what opens today.

Output becomes a written sentence with the stake stated — who, what, where, why a funder cares — not a calendar row. The model writes it; the facts come from the source.

Altitude gate is explicit: national or international significance, or genuinely singular (a reinterment at Mount Herzl qualifies; a chapter book club never does).

Major Gifts · relevance first

Invert the pipeline. Jewish or Israel relevance becomes the gate; dollar size becomes the sort within what passes. The Memphis Zoo never appears again.

Vocabulary expands from a dozen words to the actual entity space: Israeli cities, Israeli hospitals (Soroka, Hadassah, Sheba, Ichilov, Rambam), Israeli universities, and the haredi world — Satmar, Belz, Bobov, Lakewood, Monsey, kollel.

Shekels and NIS become first-class amounts, not grounds for rejection. Non-numeric gifts ("hundreds of thousands", "seven figures") stop scoring zero.

Both · measured against their archive

The test set is now built: 184 items across 60 editions, and the same can be done for Major Gifts. Every change gets scored on what share of real published items it would have surfaced.

Dedupe against eJP's own RSS, so the digest never suggests something they already published.

Target: reach 70%+ of the archive items before this posts to a live channel. Today's design tops out near 22%.

The process change that matters

Backtest before building, not after complaining

Everything above was discoverable on day one. The lesson isn't "add Israeli sources" — it's that I shipped two digests without ever checking my output against the thing it was meant to replace, when a copy of the thing it was meant to replace was sitting in a public API I had already scraped 1,204 times.

So the rule going forward, for this and anything else I build for eJP: find the existing human output, turn it into a scored test set, and report the score before asking anyone to look at a digest. "Here are 30 events I found" is not a result. "Here are 30 events, which is 74% of what you published that week, and here are the 8 I missed and why" is.

What I'd tell you not to expect

Even done properly, some fraction of Major Gifts arrives in the editor's inbox from organizations that want coverage, and some comes from his own sources. The Satmar item looks like one of those. The honest goal is to remove the tedium — the morning of Googling — and to catch the Israeli and haredi items he currently has no systematic way to see. Not to replace his judgment or his phone.

Nothing has posted to either Slack channel. Both jobs run in dry-run mode into the GitHub Actions log, and I'd keep them there until the backtest number is respectable.
